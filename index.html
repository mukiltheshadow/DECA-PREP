Individual or team: what changes

Both formats test the same thing: can you solve a business problem and explain it clearly to a judge who plays the role of a client or boss?

Times and scoring details can change each season. Check your event’s guidelines on deca.org before you compete.

A simple weekly plan

Short, steady practice beats cramming before a competition.

Two roleplay rounds

Do each in full time. Use the prep notes outline, then score yourself honestly.

Two test rounds

Play a 20-question round, read every explanation, then use Retry missed.

Five minutes of vocab, daily

Flip flashcards and say each definition in your own words before you reveal it.

Open and close strong

  • Greet the judge, say your role, and state the problem in one sentence.
  • Give your recommendation early, then explain it.
  • Close by summarizing, naming next steps, and inviting questions.

Handle judge questions

  • Pause, answer directly, and tie your answer back to the scenario.
  • If you are unsure, reason it out loud instead of guessing.
  • Judges often ask about risks or costs. Plan an answer for both.

Team roleplay tips

Team events reward partners who sound like one company, not two people taking turns.

Split by PI, not by time

Give each partner specific indicators to own so nothing gets covered twice or missed.

Practice the handoff

End your section with a bridge line like “Next, Jordan will cover the budget.” Smooth handoffs show teamwork.

Share the Q&A

Either partner can answer. Add to your partner’s answer instead of repeating it.

Use all your prep time

Team prep is longer. Spend the extra time on a shared outline and on who says what.

Individual? Own the room

With no partner, your pace matters. Speak slowly, use pauses, and watch the clock on a visible timer.

Study the real thing

Download past roleplays and rubrics from deca.org and score yourself using the real indicators.

Test prep

The DECA exam is multiple choice and covers business basics across clusters. Answer a round of practice questions, read why each answer is right, and note the vocabulary you miss.

Read the whole question

Words like “best,” “first,” and “except” change the answer. Underline them.

Eliminate two choices

Cross out answers you know are wrong, then pick between what remains.

Keep a miss list

Write down every term you miss and review that list before your next round.
